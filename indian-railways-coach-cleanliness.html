Understanding Indian train coach and toilet cleanliness ratings

A cleanliness score is useful only when you know what was rated, how many people reported it and how recently they travelled.

Published 28 July 2026 · Reviewed by the RailHygiene team

Looking for a specific service?Search by train number or name. Train pages show community observations for the overall coach, floor, toilets and dustbins when data is available.

What a train cleanliness rating can tell you

RailHygiene organises historical passenger feedback around practical parts of the journey. The overall coach score reflects the general impression of the reported coach. Separate floor, toilet and dustbin scores help reveal a problem that a single average might hide. Where possible, coach-level rows show which coach identifiers were actually reported.

This is different from a railway inspection, sanitation certificate or live status. A passenger may board after a cleaning cycle—or just before the next one. Conditions can change across coaches on the same train and across different dates. The ratings should therefore answer “what have passengers previously observed?” rather than “what is guaranteed today?”

Four checks before trusting a score

  1. Report count: one report is an early signal, not a consensus. Give more weight to patterns supported by multiple journeys.
  2. Recency: compare the latest recorded journey date with your planned travel date. Older evidence may no longer represent the service.
  3. Coach coverage: a rating for one coach does not automatically describe every coach in the rake.
  4. Category detail: read toilet, floor and dustbin values separately instead of relying only on the overall average.
Useful interpretation: a low toilet score with a better overall coach score suggests a specific sanitation concern. Several low categories across several reports suggest a broader pattern, but still not a live guarantee.

Can you check cleanliness before booking on IRCTC?

IRCTC handles railway ticketing services; RailHygiene is an independent community-rating platform. You can search RailHygiene by train number before booking or boarding to see whether historical reports exist. The result should be one input among schedule, route, class, availability and your own travel needs—not the sole basis for a booking decision.

If your ticket is already booked, the RailHygiene Android app can use a valid PNR to create a journey card and connect feedback to the relevant train and coach. Read the PNR and train cleanliness guide for privacy and usage details.

What if no reports exist for your train?

An empty result means RailHygiene lacks sufficient community data; it does not mean the train is clean or dirty. The public directory maintains train route records independently of the feedback snapshot, so a train can appear in search before it has a rating. Empty train pages are marked not to appear in search-engine results until feedback exists.

If you travel on that service, a structured rating can establish the first evidence point. Be accurate, rate only the coach you experienced and avoid using a cleanliness review to report emergencies or request immediate action.

What to do when the coach is dirty now

For a current cleanliness problem, use the official RailMadad portal or railway helpline 139. RailHygiene does not forward complaints or dispatch staff. Our dirty train coach reporting guide explains the distinction and links to official sources.
